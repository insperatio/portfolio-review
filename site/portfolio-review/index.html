A free portfolio review that tells you what to fix

An agent skill that reads your design portfolio the way an experienced reviewer would, scores it out of 100, and gives you at most five ranked fixes. No signup, no account, no email required.

Install it

npx skills add insperatio/portfolio-review

Needs Node.js. Works with Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and other agents the skills CLI supports.

Then ask:

Review my portfolio at example.com. I'm targeting a Senior Product Designer role at a B2B SaaS company.

Tell it the role you want. The review is sharper when it knows what you are aiming for, and it will ask if you leave it out.

Using Claude.ai instead

Download the repo, keep the skills/mj-portfolio-review folder, zip that folder on its own, then upload it under Customize > Skills. You will also need Code execution turned on in Settings > Capabilities. Claude.ai cannot open a page in a browser, so the review runs in source-only mode and scores out of 65. See below.

Six areas, 100 points.

85 and up, send it. 70 to 84, send after fixes. 50 to 69, fix before sending. Under 50, rework.

The metrics audit

Every number your portfolio claims gets checked for four things: a baseline, a timeframe, a data source, and your share of the result. "Increased conversion 34%" with none of those is the most common thing that falls apart when an interviewer asks about it. You get a table showing exactly which columns are empty.

The level read

You tell it the role you are targeting. It tells you what your portfolio currently reads as, where the gap is, and which project you already have is closest to closing it.

Accessibility

It measures text contrast against 4.5:1, large text and interface elements against 3:1, then checks alt text, heading order, and focus states. It reports what it measured and what passed. It does not say "ADA compliant," because the ADA does not set contrast ratios.

Two modes, stated up front

Spacing and contrast cannot be judged from source code, and many portfolios are JavaScript applications that return an empty page to a plain fetch. So every report says which mode it ran in.

  • Rendered review. A browser loaded your page and could read its colors. All 100 points are scored.
  • Source-only review. No browser available. The four content areas are scored, out of 65, and visual craft and accessibility move to open checks.

In every mode, a check that was not run is never scored. Its points come out of the total instead of being awarded, so a review that measures nothing cannot rate a portfolio higher than one that measures everything and finds real failures.

What it will not do

  • Guess your seniority from how polished your site looks.
  • Treat a well-known client as proof the work was good.
  • Ask you for longer case studies, more projects, or more process diagrams.
  • Suggest you invented a metric. A missing number is a gap, not dishonesty.
  • Make your portfolio generic. Choices that make it yours get listed under "worth keeping" so the fixes do not sand them off.

Privacy

Your portfolio URL is never collected automatically.

Two things are counted at the end of each review: the date, and which AI assistant ran it. That is the whole thing, and it exists so I know whether anyone uses this.

Never collected: your portfolio URL, your score, the findings, the fixes, your target role, your seniority, your name, your email, or any part of the conversation. Your URL identifies you and your whole work history, so the skill does not take it.

To turn counting off, blank out usage_form_id in the skill's references/tracking.json. On Claude.ai nothing is sent at all, because that environment cannot make outbound requests.

Feedback is separate and voluntary. Every report ends with a link to a form where you can say what would make this better, and optionally leave your URL, a name and an email. The code that does all of this is in the repo, so you can check this description rather than trust it.

Questions

Do I need to be technical to use this?

Right now, yes, more than I would like. The install assumes Node.js and a terminal, which is backwards for a tool built for designers. If that is a barrier, message me and I will run it on your portfolio and send you the report.

What if my portfolio is password protected?

Include the password with your request. Without it the review stops and asks, rather than scoring you on a login page. It will also note in the report that the site needs a password, because that stops real reviewers too.

Will it rewrite my case studies?

Only if you ask. The default report names what to change and where, and quotes your own words back rather than inventing replacement copy. Facts only you can confirm, like what you personally owned or where a number came from, go in a "need from you" list for you to answer.

Is a low score bad news?

It is a list of things to fix. The score judges a website against what reviewers say they look for. It does not judge your talent, your work, or whether anyone should hire you, and it cannot see what you actually did on any project.

Who made this and why is it free?

I am a senior UX and product designer and I have reviewed a lot of portfolios. This is the review I give, written down. It is MIT licensed, so you can read it, fork it, or change the rubric if you disagree with my weights.
